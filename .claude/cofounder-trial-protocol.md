# 4-Week Cofounder Trial Protocol — Korrin × Quinn
_Generated 2026-05-19 by cofounder-fit (Startup Skills)_
_Status: ACTIVE TRIAL_
_Trial window: 4 structured weeks of deliverables, embedded inside a longer 4-month summer (June–August 2026) before the Operating Agreement is signed by 2026-09-01._

## Why this exists

YC's non-negotiable rule #1: **work together for at least 4 weeks on a meaningful deliverable before committing.** Coffee chats don't count. Code-only collaboration doesn't count. What counts is shipping under pressure, running customer-facing failure, and having the hard conversation while it still costs nothing to walk away.

You've already shipped a product together (the 12-wave platform). What you have not done: gone through structured customer-facing disaster + the explicit "what if it doesn't work" conversation. This protocol forces both.

## How to use this doc

- Each week has one **deliverable** (a thing that ships) and one **observation focus** (what to watch in each other).
- At the end of each week, **both founders write a 1-paragraph private debrief** (who carried what, what frustrated me, what surprised me) and then read each other's. Reading without rebutting matters — the disagreements are the signal.
- The Week-4 hard conversation has a fixed agenda. Don't dilute it.
- If at any point either founder concludes "this isn't right," walk. Trial cost = 4 weeks. Bad-cofounder cost = the company.

## Week 1 — Ship one concrete thing together that requires both your skill sets

**Deliverable:** Pick ONE of:

- **A.** A complete case-study page on the new (rebranded) site featuring the Gatton Games coverage. Korrin curates the 25–30 image gallery + writes the event narrative. Quinn ships the page + ensures the photo pipeline + SEO + image performance is right. Live and shareable by end of week.
- **B.** A landing page + first batch of 20 cold outreach DMs to potential summer subjects (Louisville-area seniors / dance studios / event organizers). Quinn builds the page + tracking; Korrin writes the outreach copy + photographs any portfolio gaps revealed by the outreach replies.
- **C.** A 3-shoot weekend test: schedule, location-scout, shoot, edit, deliver to 3 separate subjects in 7 days under the new brand identity (even pre-formal-launch). Both involved in every step.

Pick A unless you already have replies stacking up — then B. C is the highest-pressure option and most diagnostic.

**Observation focus:**
- Who initiates? (Who says "let's start" vs. "what's next?")
- Who follows through when blocked? (Who hits the wall and stops vs. routes around?)
- Who edits whose work without ego damage?
- How do you escalate when something breaks? Calmly? Defensively? Avoidantly?

**Red flags this week:** silent disagreement on creative direction; one founder doing 80%+ of the work; either of you saying "I'll just do it myself" more than once.

## Week 2 — Customer-facing work, separately interpreted

**Deliverable:** Both of you on the same 3 customer conversations (subjects, parents, potential chapter contacts if you have any pre-Bama leads). Same call. Both listening. Then split immediately and write your own interpretation of each call separately — no comparing notes until both are written.

If you don't have 3 customer conversations queued: book them this week. Use your existing network. "Hi — we're launching a new student photography brand this summer. Would you give me 20 minutes of feedback on our positioning and pricing?" Three calls. Both on.

**Observation focus:**
- After writing separately, compare. Did you hear the same call? **Radically different interpretations of the same conversation, especially if neither of you reconciles, is the loudest warning sign cofounder-fit produces.**
- Who took notes? Who steered the conversation? Who let silence sit? Who interrupted? Who summarized?
- Did you reconcile productively after seeing each other's debrief? Or did one of you defend their interpretation?

**Red flags:** "I don't agree with how you read that call" → no engagement → topic dropped. Means you'll never align on customer signal at higher stakes.

## Week 3 — Run a real failure together

**Deliverable:** Pick something that has real chance of breaking. Suggestions:

- A pitch / quote to a Louisville-area event organizer (dance studio recital, small graduation party, large family reunion) that you might lose. Send the quote. Watch the loss or the win. Either way is data.
- A technical deadline under pressure — ship a new feature or repair a real bug while a paid shoot is happening the same weekend.
- A genuinely uncomfortable creative disagreement: pick a portfolio crop or a copy headline where you each disagree. Resolve it together in real time.

**Observation focus:**
- Behavior when things go sideways. Does your cofounder blame? Withdraw? Take ownership? Get sharp? Get vague? Step up?
- Recovery speed. How long from "we lost / it broke" to "here's what we do next"? Hours? Days? Never?
- The phrase to watch: "I told you so." If either of you uses it (even gently), that's a corrosive pattern that will get worse under bigger stakes.

**Red flags:** withdrawing into silence after failure; blaming the other person before assessing the situation; refusing to debrief because "let's just move on."

## Week 4 — The hard conversation

Schedule a 2-hour block. Phones away. Write down the answers to every question below before deciding any of them in real time.

**Agenda — answer in this order:**

1. **Trial verdict.** Independently, on a piece of paper: "Do I want to cofound with this person for 4 years?" Yes / No / Need more time. Reveal simultaneously. If anyone wrote No, the trial outcome is No. Walk away clean. The company dissolves; nobody owes anyone anything because nothing was signed.
2. **Equity.** Recommended starting point per cofounder-fit diagnosis: **50/50** equity ownership, with role-based decision tiebreakers (Korrin = brand/creative; Quinn = tech/ops; strategic = both agree, 30-day cooling period for stalemate). Deviate only with explicit reason both can defend in one sentence.
3. **Vesting.** 4-year vest, 1-year cliff. Non-negotiable. Either of you refusing this isn't a cofounder.
4. **Labor compensation.** Shooter takes 60–70% of session-net before any profit calculation. Engineer/operator (Quinn) gets fixed monthly stipend once net revenue > $1,500/mo. Codify the exact percentages now.
5. **Runway.** Each of you state aloud: "If this company makes $0 in revenue for 12 months, can I keep contributing at my stated commitment level?" Korrin's answer + Quinn's answer must both be Yes for the trial to convert to a real agreement.
6. **Time-commitment alignment.** Each of you write the hours/week you can realistically commit during fall semester (with classes, social, rush if applicable). Compare numbers. If they're off by more than 2×, you have a misalignment to solve before signing.
7. **Departure clauses.** What happens if one of you wants out at month 8? Month 18? Month 30? Walk through each. Cover: unvested-share forfeiture, ROFR on equity sales, who keeps which IP on dissolution (brand to Korrin, platform to Quinn, contact list destroyed).
8. **Life events.** What pulls one of you away with no notice? Family illness, parent objection, transferring schools, a partner emergency, a sudden internship offer, a different startup invitation, mental health. Name the most likely one for each of you. Decide now how the other one is allowed to respond.
9. **Hidden veto.** Each of you: "Is there anything I'm holding back that would change my answer to question 1 if I said it out loud?" If yes, say it now. Silent veto kills startups.

**Outcome of week 4:**
- All Yeses + alignment on 2–9 → sign the **Operating Agreement** (drafted in parallel during weeks 2–3 from Cooley GO template + $200 attorney review) by 2026-09-01.
- Any No or unreconciled gap → either extend trial 2 more weeks with a specific resolution target, or walk away clean.

## Extended trial — summer-long version (your actual situation)

You're not on a 4-week clock; you have ~14 weeks (mid-May → end of August). Use the weekly protocol above for the first 4 weeks (June), then run **monthly cadence** for July and August:

### July monthly check-in (end of July)
- Revenue to date (any new paid clients under new brand)
- Portfolio depth added since brand relaunch (count of new keeper sets)
- Outreach activity: how many DMs / cold contacts each of you initiated this month
- One thing that frustrated you about the other this month
- One thing the other did better than expected
- Honest: are you still at "Yes" on question 1?

### August monthly check-in (mid-August, before Bama move)
- Same revenue / portfolio / outreach metrics
- Bama prep status: chapter target list built? Outreach scripts drafted? Move logistics handled?
- Time-commitment reality check: classes start in ~2 weeks; recalibrate the fall hours/week number
- Sign the **Operating Agreement** by 2026-09-01 OR formally extend with a written reason and new gate date

### MoU (sign this week — before any paid client at the new brand)

One page, both signatures, dated. Covers:

1. We are in a structured cofounder trial from [start date] through 2026-09-01.
2. During the trial, all revenue from the business goes into a shared business bank account, both signers required, no draws.
3. Expenses (insurance, LLC fees, gear, software) are tracked and reimbursed from the business account; founders track personal contributions to be reconciled at trial end.
4. No equity changes hands during the trial. Neither founder owns any percentage of the business until the Operating Agreement is signed.
5. The trial converts to the Operating Agreement on 2026-09-01 if both founders agree.
6. If the trial does not convert: brand IP (name, logo, social handles, domain) returns to Korrin; platform IP (code, infrastructure, schema) returns to Quinn; client contact list is destroyed (not shared); any pending bookings are referred to the booked client and either cofounder can choose to honor the booking under their own name.
7. Either founder may terminate the trial with 7 days' written notice. Termination triggers section 6.

That's it. One page. Both initial each clause. Both sign. Date. Save scanned copy to the project.

## What this protocol prevents

- Showing up at Bama with no agreement → first big disagreement → company dissolves in a week with no IP clarity → both walk away with nothing usable.
- Korrin doing 100% of shoots → resentful at month 8 → drops out → Quinn left with platform tied to a dead brand.
- Quinn taking on a job offer in October → Korrin left to run everything → no operating agreement to fall back on → either she pays him for past work or he sues.
- Either of you discovering at month 6 that you assumed different things about decision rights, IP ownership, or pace of growth.
